Program Architecture

Problem, Goal & Expected Outcomes

The Problem

Civic infrastructure and environmental maintenance complaints across Sri Lanka often stall between disconnected hotlines, paper logbooks, and overlapping local council boundaries.

  • No Location Precision: Verbal street descriptions delay municipal field crew dispatch.
  • Zero Citizen Visibility: Residents rarely receive a reference code or know which officer owns the task.
  • Reporting Hesitation: Citizens fear retaliation when reporting illegal dumping without identity protection.

The Goal

Deliver a single, transparent digital intake and dispatch system under Clean Sri Lanka—linking every citizen report directly to the responsible Province, District, and Municipal Council.

  • Structured Intake: Photo evidence, standardized categories, and interactive GPS pinpoints.
  • Guaranteed Reference IDs: Every submission generates an immutable CSL-2026-XXXX code.
  • End-to-End Accountability: Four auditable lifecycle stages from intake through council resolution.

Key Outcomes

Standardized triage and real-time analytics empower municipal commissioners and engineering units to resolve high-impact civic hazards faster and allocate resources where demand peaks.

  • Faster Dispatch: Exact coordinates route crews directly to blocked drains, waste dumps, and hazards.
  • Protected Whistleblowing: One-click anonymous toggle keeps citizen identity confidential while preserving tracking.
  • Data-Driven Governance: Live council metrics highlight bottlenecks by province and maintenance category.
Transparent Workflow

Four-Stage Resolution Lifecycle

STAGE 01 Received Complaint validated, GPS coordinates locked, and unique reference number issued.
STAGE 02 Assigned Municipal Council supervisor assigns a named Field Officer or engineering unit.
STAGE 03 In Progress Crew dispatched on-site; cleanup, repair, or hazard mitigation actively underway.
STAGE 04 Resolved Work verified complete, closure logged, and citizen notification status updated.
